Core idea:
Portfolio Writing Direction

The writing should be simple, technical, honest, and restrained.

The portfolio is not a résumé, a marketing page, or a collection of claims. It is a professional presentation of an ongoing technical practice.

The central idea is:

«I build things, investigate how they work, and document what I learn.»

Everything should support that idea.

- Home introduces who I am, what I work on, and where the work leads.
- Workbench presents the actual projects, experiments, and investigations.
- About provides the background, experience, education, and context behind the work.
- Contact provides a simple path to start a conversation.

The writing should show rather than sell. Technical ability should be demonstrated through the work, not exaggerated through words like expert, highly skilled, or passionate.

Cybersecurity is the primary focus, but the writing should leave room for systems, networking, software, Linux, hardware, electronics, and whatever I explore next.

The tone should be:

«Clear. Technical. Calm. Direct. Curious. Professional.»

Every section should say what needs to be said, then stop.

Why the Metadata Uses Third Person

The visible portfolio uses first person because it is Shedrack speaking directly to the visitor:


I build things, investigate how they work, and document what I learn.



Metadata serves a different purpose. It describes who and what the site is for search engines, link previews, indexing, and other external contexts.

Therefore:


Website copy → first person

Metadata / SEO → third person



This keeps the site personal while making its external description clear, neutral, and professional..........
